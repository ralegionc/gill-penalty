Why about one kelvin

Each mL of oxygen burned releases about 19.6 J. A mL of blood carries at most its oxygen capacity, and warms by 1 K for every 3.7 J. Blood that left the gills at sea temperature can therefore be warmed by at most 19.6 × capacity / 3.7 kelvin before it returns, about 1 K for the richest blood. Body size and effort cancel out: a bigger or busier animal burns more oxygen and must push proportionally more blood past its gills.

Heat crosses the gill wall thousands of times more readily than oxygen does, so no gill can load oxygen without first dumping the heat.

How the numbers are made

Metabolism is an endotherm's: 2.5 times Kleiber's basal rate. Gill area uses the highest oxygen uptake per square metre of gill recorded in FishBase (5,300 mg per m² per hour, active white sucker at 20 °C); a gill extracts 60% of the oxygen in the water. Lungs warm and humidify air taken in at the water's temperature and remove 6% of its volume as oxygen. Skin, fat and nasal heat exchange are left out, so the lung figures are upper bounds and the gill figures assume a perfect insulator.
